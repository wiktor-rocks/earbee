Looking for a free AnthemScore alternative? First, a plain answer: if you want a free app that turns a recording into sheet music by itself, EarBee isn't it. It doesn't transcribe anything automatically. What it does is help you work the song out by ear: [EarBee](/) is a free, open-source player for Windows, Mac and Linux that slows a song down, loops a passage and finds the beats and bars. I make it, so weigh my opinion accordingly.

Below: what AnthemScore does and costs, whether there's a free version, how the two compare, and when working it out by ear beats an automatic guess.

## What is AnthemScore?

[AnthemScore](https://www.lunaverus.com/) is a desktop app from Lunaverus that, in its own words, "converts audio to sheet music using AI". You open an MP3 or a WAV, and it writes out the notes as sheet music, MIDI or MusicXML. It also tries to detect the beats, the drums, the chord symbols and which instruments are playing, and it can write guitar and bass parts as tablature ([its FAQ](https://www.lunaverus.com/faq)).

After that comes the editing. It shows the song as a spectrogram, with the detected notes on top, and you add, remove and move notes until the score matches what you hear ([its documentation](https://www.lunaverus.com/documentation)).

## How much does AnthemScore cost?

It's a one-time purchase, not a subscription. In November 2026, [three editions](https://www.lunaverus.com/purchase) were on sale:

- **Lite, $29:** full songs, with the sheet music to view, save and print.
- **Professional, $39:** adds the export (PDF, MIDI, MusicXML), the note editing tools and the playback controls, such as slowing down.
- **Studio, $99:** adds batch processing and lifetime updates.

Lite and Professional come with a year of updates. After that you keep the version you have, or pay to renew. The [comparison page](https://www.lunaverus.com/compareEditions) has the details.

## Is there a free version of AnthemScore?

Not a full one. There are two free ways to try it:

- **The trial** of the desktop app transcribes the first 30 seconds of each song, up to 100 songs in total ([the download page](https://www.lunaverus.com/download)). No account is needed.
- **The web version**, on [the home page](https://www.lunaverus.com/), does unlimited 30-second clips for free; full songs need a subscription.

So if you only need the opening of a song, the free trial may be all you need. For whole songs, you pay.

## Is AnthemScore safe?

The copy from [its own download page](https://www.lunaverus.com/download) is the one to trust. Copies from download sites, and "free" cracked versions, are where the risk is. If a free AnthemScore turns up somewhere other than lunaverus.com, I'd leave it alone.

## Why EarBee doesn't transcribe for you

Because it's made for the other way of doing it. An automatic transcription is like a translation app: handy when you need to read the menu tonight, but it won't teach you the language. When you work a phrase out yourself, your ear gets a little better at the next one, and that's the part I care about. There's more on this in [why transcribing by ear makes you a better musician](/blog/why-transcribe-music-by-ear/).

That's my view, and you don't have to share it. If what you need is a score on paper by Friday, an automatic tool is a fair choice, and AnthemScore is one of those.

## When working it out by ear is better

An automatic transcription is a guess, and you check a guess by listening. That's a skill in itself, and it's the same one you'd use to work the song out from scratch. Some songs make the guessing harder than others:

- **Bent notes, slides and vibrato,** which sit between the notes a score can write.
- **Songs that aren't tuned to standard pitch.** AnthemScore's [FAQ](https://www.lunaverus.com/faq) says it can't yet process those accurately; older recordings are often slightly off.
- **Busy mixes,** where a chord is spread across three instruments and the bass sits under the kick drum.
- **Feel:** how far behind the beat a note lands, which notes are ghosted and which are leaned on. A score gets the notes and loses most of that.

On a song like that, you'll be listening closely anyway, and doing it from the start can be quicker than fixing a long list of wrong notes.

## How to work a song out by ear

1. **Map the song first:** where the verses, choruses and solo are, so you know what you're working on.
2. **Loop one short phrase,** a bar or two.
3. **Slow it down** until every note is clear. Here's [how to slow down a song without changing the pitch](/blog/slow-down-a-song-without-changing-pitch/).
4. **Find the first note,** sing it, then find it on your instrument. For the bass line, here's [how to hear the bass in a song](/blog/hear-the-bass-in-a-song/).
5. **Write it down** in whatever you use, tab, notation or a notebook, and move the loop on to the next phrase.
6. **Play along at full speed** to check.

If you're starting out, pick a song where this is easy: here are [eight easy songs to transcribe](/blog/easy-songs-to-transcribe/).

## What EarBee adds

### You can see where you are

EarBee shows the song as a waveform, and you loop a passage by dragging across it. When you've got it, you move the loop on instead of setting it again from scratch.

### Loops that fit the music

EarBee can find the beats and bars of a song, on your computer, and draw them on the waveform. Then one key loops the bar you're in. Here's [why the bars matter more than the tempo](/blog/find-the-bpm-of-a-song-tap-tempo/).

### Video, and YouTube

AnthemScore opens audio files. EarBee opens those, and video files too, with the picture in sync at any speed, so at half speed you see the fingers at half speed. Paste a YouTube link, or search by name, and it opens the video, or only its sound.

### A map of the song, and your work kept

You can mark each section, give it a name and a colour, and jump between them. A project file keeps your marks, your loop, the speed and the EQ, so tomorrow you start where you stopped.

## What AnthemScore does better

To be fair to it:

- **It writes the score for you.** If you need sheet music, MIDI or a tab as a file, EarBee won't make one: you write it yourself.
- **Drums and chords,** detected and written out.
- **A note editor on a spectrogram,** where you can see each note's pitch as well as hear it.
- **Batch processing,** in Studio: a folder of songs transcribed in one go.

## Is AnthemScore worth it?

It depends on what you want at the end. If it's a file, sheet music or MIDI to hand to someone, it can save you hours, and the free trial lets you see how well it does on your kind of music before you pay. If it's being able to play the song, and to work out the next one faster, the work you skip is the work that teaches you.

## Which one would I pick?

- **You need sheet music or MIDI, and you need it soon:** AnthemScore. Try the free trial first, on the music you actually play.
- **You want to learn songs by ear, with slowing down, loops and video:** EarBee.
- **Both:** they don't get in each other's way. Use AnthemScore for a first draft, and EarBee to slow down and loop the bars where the draft sounds wrong.
