To transcribe a song by ear is to work out what's played on a recording by listening, then play it back or write it down. The method, in short: map the song's sections, find the key and the beat, then work out the bass, the chords and the melody, one short passage at a time, and check it all by playing along. Below: each step, how long it takes, and the tools.

_To write: one or two sentences in your voice, as in the other posts. Why the whole song and not just a riff, and a link to [why transcribing makes you a better musician](/blog/why-transcribe-music-by-ear/) for the reasons. That post's "How to start" is the short version for a single phrase; this one is the whole song._

## 1. Pick the song, and what you'll take from it

_To write: something you know well and like. Decide what you're after: the chords to play along, the bass line, the solo, or all of it. For a first song, link [easy songs to transcribe](/blog/easy-songs-to-transcribe/)._

## 2. Listen through and map its sections

_To write: listen without your instrument; mark the intro, verses, choruses and bridge with their times. Repeats save work: a second chorus is usually the first one again (say "usually"). Analogy idea: a map before a hike._

## 3. Find the key and the beat

_To write: the key centre, from the note the song feels at home on (often the last chord). The beat and the bars: tap along and count; link [how to find the BPM of a song](/blog/find-the-bpm-of-a-song-tap-tempo/). Why the bars matter: they tell you where the chords change._

## 4. Start with the bass

_To write: the lowest notes usually give each chord's root. Hum it, find it on your instrument, one bar at a time. An EQ that brings the low end forward helps (in general terms)._

## 5. Work out the chords

_To write: over each bass note, major or minor first, then anything extra. Play it along with the recording: if it clashes, it's wrong. The easy songs post is the practice for this step._

## 6. Then the melody, the riffs and the solo

_To write: loop one phrase, sing it before you play it, find the first note, then the rest. Slow it down when notes blur; link [how to slow down a song without changing the pitch](/blog/slow-down-a-song-without-changing-pitch/)._

## 7. Write it down, or don't

_To write: your view. Tabs, a chord chart, notation, or nothing. **For you to decide:** the "why" post says writing it down is optional ("plenty of players never write a note"); the ear training post says to write down at least some of it. Pick one line, so the two posts agree._

## 8. Check it by playing along

_To write: at full speed, with the recording; go back to any passage that sounds off. The part you got wrong is the one you'll remember._

## How long does it take to transcribe a song?

_To write (a searched question): an honest range, in your words, from your experience. It depends on the song, the part and how much you've done. The first one is slow, the next ones go faster._

## Is there a free way to transcribe music?

_To write (Google's "People also ask"): yes, your ears and a free player that slows down and loops. Link [free alternatives to Transcribe!](/blog/free-alternative-to-transcribe/)._

## Can AI transcribe a song for you?

_To write (Google's "People also ask", and most of its ads): short and fair. It can try, and it's useful for a quick chart; but you don't learn anything when it does the work, like a calculator and sums. Link the "why" post's answer instead of repeating it._
