Looking for a free alternative to Amazing Slow Downer? I'd suggest [EarBee](/), a free, open-source player for Mac, Windows and Linux. I make it, so weigh my opinion accordingly. It slows music down without changing the pitch, like Amazing Slow Downer, and then helps with the rest of the work: seeing where you are in the song, looping it bar by bar, and marking the parts you've learned. Amazing Slow Downer still does a few things EarBee doesn't, and I'll be fair about those too.

Below: what Amazing Slow Downer is and costs, how the two compare, and which one I'd pick for what.

## What Amazing Slow Downer is, and what it costs

[Amazing Slow Downer](https://www.ronimusic.com/slowdown.htm), from Roni Music, has been around since the late 1990s, and it's one of the best-known tools for practising with recordings. It does what its name says, and does it well: it plays a song slower or faster without changing the pitch. You can loop a passage, shift the pitch in semitones and cents to match your tuning, shape the sound with an EQ and turn down the vocals. On a computer, a foot pedal or a MIDI controller can run it.

It comes in four versions, each bought separately:

- **Windows and Mac:** [$39.95](https://www.ronimusic.com/register.htm) each, paid once, after a free trial.
- **iPhone and iPad:** about $15, with a free Lite version that plays only part of each song.
- **Android:** [$9.95](https://www.ronimusic.com/asd_android.htm), also with a free Lite version.

That's a fair price for a tool that has been looked after for so long. But if you practise at a computer, you can do the same for free.

## Slowing down is only one step

Slowing a song down is like a magnifying glass: it makes the detail big enough to see. But nobody learns a song with a magnifying glass alone. You also need to know where you are, go back to the same spot again and again, and remember what you've already worked out.

Think of how you'd work out a solo. You listen to the whole thing first and find where it starts and ends. You loop the first phrase, slow it down until you can sing it, and find it on your instrument. Then you move on to the next phrase, and the next, and come back tomorrow to the bit that beat you. Slowing down helps with one of those steps. EarBee is built around all of them.

## What EarBee adds

### You can see where you are

EarBee shows the song as a waveform, so you can see where a phrase starts, where the drums hit and where the quiet bit ends. You loop a passage by dragging across it. When you've got it, you move the loop on to the next phrase instead of setting it again from scratch. It sounds like a small thing, but you'll do it hundreds of times in one song.

### Loops that fit the music

EarBee can find the beats and bars of a song, on your computer, and draw them on the waveform. Then one key loops the bar you're in, and others double it, halve it or move it on to the next bar. Music is made of bars, so it makes sense to learn it bar by bar: one bar, then the next, then the two together.

### A map of the song

Before you learn a song, it helps to know its shape: intro, verse, chorus, solo. In EarBee you mark each section, give it a name and a colour, and jump between them. A long song stops being one long blur and becomes a handful of parts, each one small enough to learn.

### Video, and YouTube

A lot of what we learn from today is on video: lessons, live performances, someone's hands filmed up close. EarBee keeps the picture in sync at any speed, so at half speed you see the fingers at half speed too. Paste a YouTube link, or search by name, and EarBee opens the video, or only its sound.

### Your work is kept

A project file keeps your marks, your loop, the speed and the EQ, so tomorrow you start where you stopped, not from the beginning.

### Hands on the keys

Learning by ear is mostly a lot of very small actions: stop, go back a little, play again, start the loop over. If every one of them needs the mouse, your hands keep leaving your instrument. In EarBee each one is a key, and one key replays from where you started listening.

## What Amazing Slow Downer does better

To be fair to it, there are good reasons to buy it:

- **Phone and tablet apps.** If you practise with your phone on a music stand, its iPhone, iPad and Android apps are the point, and they cost less than the computer version. EarBee runs on computers only.
- **Foot pedal and MIDI control.** Start, stop and loop without taking your hands off your instrument at all.
- **Vocal removal,** to turn the singer down and sing or play the part yourself.
- **Speeding up as you go.** It can raise the speed a little each time the loop comes round, so you work your way up to full tempo without stopping. In EarBee you change the speed yourself.
- **Audio CDs.** It plays a CD straight from the drive. EarBee opens files.

If any of these matter to you, try its trial alongside EarBee, on the same song, and see which one you keep reaching for.

## Moving over from Amazing Slow Downer

If you're used to Amazing Slow Downer, here's the same routine in EarBee:

1. **Open the song.** Drop an audio or video file on the window, or paste a YouTube link.
2. **Map it.** Listen once at full speed and mark the sections you want to learn.
3. **Loop a passage.** Drag across the waveform, or find the beats and loop one bar.
4. **Slow it down** until you can play it cleanly. How slow to go, and why very slow speeds sound strange, is in [how to slow down a song without changing the pitch](/blog/slow-down-a-song-without-changing-pitch/).
5. **Match your tuning** if the band tuned down: shift the pitch in semitones, or in cents for an old recording that's slightly off.
6. **Bring it back up to speed** a step at a time, then move the loop on to the next phrase.
7. **Save a project,** so your marks, loop and speed are there next time.

## Is there a free Amazing Slow Downer for Mac?

Not the full version: the free Lite versions are for phones and tablets. EarBee runs on Macs with Apple Silicon and on Intel Macs, and it's free there as it is on Windows and Linux. Amazing Slow Downer's Mac version is also a separate purchase from its Windows one, so if you use both systems, EarBee saves you paying twice.

## What about Spotify?

Amazing Slow Downer's phone apps could once slow down songs from Spotify, until Spotify stopped allowing it in September 2022. Its computer versions don't play Spotify or Apple Music at all. EarBee doesn't either, but most songs on Spotify are on YouTube too: paste the link, or search for the song by name, and EarBee opens it, ready to slow down and loop like any other song.

## Which one would I pick?

- **You practise on a phone or tablet, or with a foot pedal:** Amazing Slow Downer.
- **You practise at a computer, and want to see the song, loop it bar by bar, mark its parts or watch a video:** EarBee.
- **You're not sure:** EarBee costs nothing, and Amazing Slow Downer has a trial. Use both on the same song for a week, and you'll know.

Whichever you choose, the tool matters less than the habit: a short passage, slowed down until you can play it cleanly, looped, then brought back up to speed.
