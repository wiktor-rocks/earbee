Looking for a Riffstation alternative? It depends on what you used it for. If you want a free app that slows a song down, shifts its pitch and loops a passage, I'd suggest [EarBee](/), a free, open-source player for Windows, Mac and Linux. I make it, so weigh my opinion accordingly. If what you want is the chords worked out for you, EarBee isn't it: it doesn't detect chords, and I'll explain why, and what you can do instead.

Below: what happened to Riffstation, how the two compare, and how to get the chords without it.

## Is Riffstation still available?

Not officially. Riffstation came from a company called Sonic Ladder and was later bought by Fender. In May 2018 [the web and mobile versions were closed](https://www.kvraudio.com/news/riffstation-pro-now-free-41355), with this message:

> While we work with labels and publishers on a paid Riffstation service, Riffstation will no longer be available on the web or mobile devices. We hope to bring a similar service back in the future, but in the meantime, we'd like to offer you a free copy of Riffstation Pro desktop app (normally $34.99), on us.

So for a while, yes, Riffstation was free. According to [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/riffstation/about), the desktop app was withdrawn too in early 2019. I couldn't find any sign of the promised service since.

Old copies still turn up on download sites. I can't tell you whether they run on a current computer, and I'd be careful about installing software from those sites.

## What Riffstation did

Its main trick was listening to a song and showing the chords as it played, in time with the music, with diagrams for the fingering. Around that were the tools you'd expect for practising with recordings: [slowing down, changing the pitch and looping](https://rekkerd.org/fender-sets-free-riffstation-pro-app-for-windows-and-mac/), a metronome synced to the song, and a way to [isolate or mute a guitar part](https://alternativeto.net/software/riffstation/about).

How good were the chords? Opinions differed. One [user](https://alternativeto.net/software/riffstation/about) called them "pretty accurate", [another](https://www.kvraudio.com/news/riffstation-pro-now-free-41355) said about 50 % were right. A [review at delamar](https://www.delamar.de/test/riffstation-testbericht/) found they worked quite well most of the time, with room to improve on chord extensions, which is about what you'd expect from software that guesses.

## Why EarBee doesn't detect chords

Because it's made for working them out yourself. A chord chart from an app is like the answers at the back of the book: fine for checking your work, or when you need the song by tonight's gig, but you don't learn much from copying them. When you find a chord yourself, your ear gets a little better at finding the next one. There's more on this in [why transcribing by ear makes you a better musician](/blog/why-transcribe-music-by-ear/).

That's my view, and you don't have to share it. If you just want the chords, you want a chord detection app, such as [Chordify](https://chordify.net), not EarBee. Whichever you use, check its answers by ear, as you had to with Riffstation.

## How to find the chords without Riffstation

It's slower at first, and it gets faster.

1. **Loop one chord change.** Two chords are easier than a whole verse.
2. **Slow it down** if the change goes past too quickly. Here's [how to slow down a song without changing the pitch](/blog/slow-down-a-song-without-changing-pitch/).
3. **Find the bass note.** Most of the time it's the root, the note the chord is named after. If it's buried, here's [how to hear the bass in a song](/blog/hear-the-bass-in-a-song/).
4. **Decide: major or minor?** Play both on that root, along with the recording. Your ear will tell you which one is wrong.
5. **Play along at full speed** to check, then move the loop on to the next change.

If you're starting out, pick a song where this is easy. Here are [eight easy songs to transcribe](/blog/easy-songs-to-transcribe/).

## What EarBee adds

### You can see where you are

EarBee shows the song as a waveform, and you loop a passage by dragging across it. When you've got it, you move the loop on instead of setting it again from scratch.

### Loops that fit the music

EarBee can find the beats and bars of a song, on your computer, and draw them on the waveform. Then one key loops the bar you're in, and since chords usually change on the bar, that's often the very loop you want. Here's [why the bars matter more than the tempo](/blog/find-the-bpm-of-a-song-tap-tempo/).

### Video, and YouTube

Riffstation Pro worked with audio files. EarBee opens those, and video files too, with the picture in sync at any speed, so at half speed you see the fingers at half speed. Paste a YouTube link, or search by name, and it opens the video, or only its sound.

### A map of the song, and your work kept

You can mark each section, give it a name and a colour, and jump between them. A project file keeps your marks, your loop, the speed and the EQ, so tomorrow you start where you stopped.

## What Riffstation did better

To be fair to it:

- **The chords, as they played,** with the fingering beside them. If that's what you miss, EarBee won't replace it.
- **Isolating a part.** Riffstation could pull forward a guitar or a voice, or mute it. EarBee's EQ can bring a range of notes forward, as with the bass, but that's not the same thing.

## Is there a Riffstation alternative for Mac or Linux?

For Mac, yes: EarBee runs on Macs, and it's free there as it is everywhere else. And if you were on Linux, Riffstation was never made for it as far as I can find, while EarBee runs there just as it does on Windows and Mac.

## Which one would I pick?

- **You want to work songs out yourself, with slowing down, loops and video:** EarBee.
- **You want the chords handed to you:** a chord detection app.
- **You have an old copy of Riffstation that still runs:** keep it, and try EarBee next to it on the same song. EarBee costs nothing, so you've nothing to lose but an evening.
